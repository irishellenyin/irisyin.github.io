I am currently a second-year PhD candidate in astrophysics at The University of Hong Kong. I started working on CubeSat development and gamma-ray burst (GRB) data analysis as an undergraduate at Nanjing University, where I earned my B.S. in Physics in 2023 and M.S. in Astronomy in 2025.

My primary research interest centers on extragalactic fast X-ray transients (EFXTs), gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) and their associated astrophysical phenomena. The focus of my recent research has been on investigating the diverse populations of EFXTs detected by the Einstein Probe (EP), including Type I (compact object merger origin), Type II (collapsar origin), and Type III (supermassive black hole and other origins) EFXTs.

As an active Transient Advocate (TA) of EP, I concentrate on the EFXTs and multi-messenger observations. I have led detailed studies of several representative EFXTs, including EP240219a, the first EFXT detected by EP associated with an untriggered GRB; EP250704a, a minutes-long Type I EFXT associated with a short GRB; and EP250304a, a supernova-associated Type II EFXT originating from a jet-cocoon system. In addition to case studies, I am conducting population analyses of Type I EFXTs to constrain the remnant type of compact object mergers, as well as systematic studies of the evolution of early X-ray absorption in EFXTs to probe their local environments.
